The Two Paths

Same Money, Two Starting Points

One asks what I can do. The other asks what I need.

Effectual · start from the means

I have ₱5,000, a building I am in five days a week, and a class group chat. What can I make out of that?

  • Decide byAffordable loss — what I can lose without it hurting.
  • The goalOpen. It gets shaped by whatever actually sells.
  • First weekSpend ₱0. Get 25 classmates to commit to a price.

Causal · start from the goal

I want ₱6,000 net profit a month by December. What has to be true, and what do I need to get there?

  • Decide byExpected return — the plan that best hits the number.
  • The goalFixed first. Resources are gathered to fit it.
  • First weekPick the market, do the math, write the steps.

Terms as defined by Saras D. Sarasvathy, "Causation and Effectuation," Academy of Management Review, 2001.

Effectual · Step 1

What I Already Have

The ₱5,000 is the smallest of the four.

  • Who I amA student in the same building, five days a week, on the same schedule as my customers. No commute cost, no rent, no permit for a stall I do not have.
  • What I knowWhich rooms have 7:30 AM classes, when the long breaks fall, and that the nearest ₱40 iced coffee is a twelve-minute walk off campus.
  • Who I knowThree class group chats I am already a member of — about 180 people who will read a message from me without me paying for reach.

What the money is actually for

₱5,000 does not buy a business. It buys one week of stock and one piece of equipment — enough to find out whether the other three means are worth anything.

"Given only this, what can I start — this week?"The effectual question
Effectual · Step 2

Three Things ₱5,000 Can Actually Start

All three use the same four means. Only the product changes.

  1. Kape sa KlasePre-ordered iced coffee, handed over at the 7:30 AM and 3:00 PM breaks, three days a week. Capital ₱3,800 · ₱40 − ₱17 = ₱23 a cup.
  2. Print RunnerI collect files in the morning, get them printed and bound off campus, hand them back before the deadline. Capital ₱1,000 · ₱25 a job.
  3. Frozen by the TrayReady-to-fry siomai and lumpia, sold by the tray to boarding-house students who cook for themselves. Capital ₱4,200 · ₱180 − ₱130 = ₱50 a tray.

Option 3 needs a freezer I do not own. Option 2 earns thin and caps out at the print shop's hours. Option 1 is the one I can run alone, on my own schedule, starting Monday.

Effectual · Step 3

I Pick Option 01 — and This Week I Spend ₱0

The first action is not buying. It is getting people to commit.

First action · this week

  1. Mon, Sep 8 — Post the offer in three group chatsOne photo, one price: ₱40 for a 16oz iced coffee, handed to you at your room before your 7:30.
  2. Tue–Wed, Sep 9–10 — Collect 25 names, not 25 likesA name on the list means "I will pay ₱40 on Monday." Reactions do not count.
  3. Thu, Sep 11 — Price the kit and ask permissionQuote the jug, cups and coffee in person. Ask my adviser what selling inside the building requires.
  4. Fri, Sep 12 — 25 names or the money stays in my pocketAt 25 or more I buy the kit on Saturday and sell Monday. Under 25 I re-price or take option 02 instead.

Effectual · Step 4

The Most I Am Willing to Lose

Not a feeling. A number, with a rule attached.

Affordable loss

₱3,800

of the ₱5,000 — the kit and the first week of stock. If all of it disappears, I have lost two weeks of allowance and no one else's money.

How the ₱5,000 splits

At risk ₱3,800₱1,200

The ₱1,200 stays untouched. It is not savings — it is the cash that lets me restock next week without borrowing.

What I will not risk

  1. Borrowed moneyNo loan, no utang from family, no 5-6. A first business that owes someone is a first business that cannot be closed.
  2. Class hoursSelling happens in the two breaks I already have free. The moment it needs a third, it waits for the semester break.
  3. More than ₱3,800, everGrowth gets funded out of margin. If the business cannot pay for its own next jug, it does not get one.

The stop rule

Three selling weeks — nine days. If gross margin has not passed ₱1,900, half of what I risked, I stop, sell the jug, and keep the ₱1,200.

Causal · The goal, worked backward

₱6,000 a Month Is 24 Cups a Day

Fix the goal first, then divide until the number is something I can watch.

  1. ₱6,000 net profit a monthThe goal, by December 2026.
  2. ₱6,400 gross margin needed₱400 covers breakage, transport and replacements.
  3. 279 cups a month₱6,400 ÷ ₱23 margin a cup = 278.3, rounded up.
  4. 24 cups a dayAcross 12 selling days — three days a week, four weeks a month.

Is 24 realistic? There are about 600 students on the three floors I sell to. 24 cups is 4 out of every 100 buying once.

Can I physically do it? Split over two breaks, that is 12 cups per window — one hand-off every 2.5 minutes, all pre-paid.

What breaks it? Margin slipping under ₱21 a cup. At ₱18 I would need 31 cups a day for the same ₱6,000.

Causal · The market

Three Floors, One Building, 7:30 AM

Chosen because I can reach all of it on foot in under two minutes.

Why this market

  1. They already buy coffeeI am not creating a habit, I am moving one. The 7:30 crowd is already caffeinated by something.
  2. The nearest option is a walkTwelve minutes off campus, or ₱60 and up on it. I sell at ₱40, inside the building, before the bell.
  3. I can see demand before I spendPre-orders close at 9 PM. I buy ice and brew for exactly the number of cups already paid for. Nothing spoils.

Primary

2nd to 4th year students with 7:30 AM classes. About 600 of them on my three floors. Same rooms every week, so the list stays warm.

Secondary

Faculty on the same floors. Fewer people, bigger ticket — ₱55 for a 22oz. Worth serving, not worth planning around.

Not my market — yet

The whole campus, walk-in buyers, and delivery. Each one needs a cart, a permit, or hours I do not have while carrying 21 units.

Causal · Strategy & execution

Four Months, Four Numbers to Hit

Each step exists only because the one after it needs it.

Pre-order only, one building, ₱40 flat — so I never buy stock I have not sold, and I never compete on anything but distance.The strategy in one line

Execution steps

  1. September — validate free, then launch25 committed names, then Mon–Wed–Fri selling.Target · 30 cups/day
  2. October — add the 3 PM window and the ₱55 largeFix the recipe so every cup tastes the same.Target · 45 cups/day
  3. November — pay a classmate ₱150 a dayThey run the morning window so I can attend my own 7:30.Target · 60 cups/day
  4. December — hold the average through finalsThen decide: a second building, or pause until January.Goal · ₱6,000 net

Checked weekly: cups sold, margin per cup, pre-order fill rate, and cash on hand — which never drops below ₱1,200.

Recap

I Used Both — in This Order

Effectual to survive week one. Causal to be worth something by December.

  1. What do I sell?Effectual: coffee, because I am in the building at 7:30 anyway. Causal: coffee, because ₱23 a cup is the only margin that reaches ₱6,000.
  2. How do I start?Effectual: one post, 25 names, ₱0 spent — buy only after they commit. Causal: silent on this. A plan does not tell you what to do on Monday.
  3. How much do I risk?Effectual: ₱3,800, with a written stop rule at nine selling days. Causal: whatever the ₱6,000 target needs — which is how plans get expensive.
  4. When am I done?Effectual: never fixed — whatever the buyers turn it into. Causal: December, ₱6,000 net, 24 cups a day. A number I can be wrong about.

Effectual reasoning got me selling in five days. Causal reasoning is the only reason I know whether selling was worth it.
