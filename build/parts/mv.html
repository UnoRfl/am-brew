The Two Paths

Same ₱5,000, Two Starting Points

  • Effectual"We have ₱5,000, an oven and five of us. What can we make?" We decide by what we can afford to lose — so the goal stays open.
  • Causal"We want ₱6,000 a month by December. What do we need to get there?" We decide by the plan that hits the number — so the goal comes first.

Terms as defined by Saras D. Sarasvathy, "Causation and Effectuation," Academy of Management Review, 2001.

Effectual · What we could start

Three Things We Can Make at Home

  1. Coffee & cookiesBottled iced coffee with a pack of homemade cookies. Start-up ₱3,500 · earns ₱35 a bottle and ₱20 a pack.
  2. Cookies onlyCheapest to start — we already have the oven. Start-up ₱1,500 · earns ₱20 a pack.
  3. Iced tea onlyCheaper to make than coffee, but it sells for less. Start-up ₱2,500 · earns ₱18 a bottle.

We pick 01. It earns the most per item, and cookies do not melt on the way to school.

Effectual · Affordable loss

The Most We Are Willing to Lose

At risk

₱3,500

The insulated bag, the bottles, and the first week of ingredients.

Out of the ₱5,000

Spent ₱3,500₱1,500

The ₱1,500 we keep is what lets us restock week two without asking anyone for money.

The stop rule

Three weeks. If we have not made back half of the ₱3,500, we stop and sell the bag.

Losing ₱700 each costs us one week of allowance. That is why we can start on Monday instead of arguing about it for a month.

Effectual · First action

This Week We Spend ₱0 Until Friday

  1. Monday — Post the offer in our five class group chatsOne photo, two prices: ₱60 a bottle, ₱35 a cookie pack. We ask for names, not likes — a name means "I am paying on Monday."
  2. Tuesday — Bake one test batch and taste it togetherAll five of us try it, then we lock the recipe and the pack size. Nothing gets sold that the group has not tasted.
  3. Wednesday — Ask permission before we sell anythingOne of us asks our adviser what selling inside the building needs. If the answer is no, we hand over at the gate instead.
  4. Thursday — Count the names: 20 orders, or we re-priceUnder 20 we buy nothing. We drop the price, or we sell cookies only, which costs ₱1,500 instead of ₱3,500.
  5. Friday — Buy the ₱3,500 kit and split the jobsTwo bake Sunday night, one bottles and chills, two hand over in the morning. We sell for the first time the Monday after.
Causal · The goal, worked backward

₱6,000 a Month Is One Bag a Day

  1. ₱6,000 a monthThe goal, by December.
  2. ₱1,500 a weekDivided over four weeks.
  3. ₱500 a dayOver three selling days — Monday, Wednesday, Friday.
  4. 10 bottles + 8 cookie packsWhich is what ₱500 a day actually looks like.

Can we carry it? Ten bottles and eight cookie packs is one insulated bag — light enough for one person to bring in.

Can we sell it? Eighteen items in one 30-minute window, all paid for the night before. Nobody queues, nothing is left over.

Causal · The market

Our Own Building, Before the 7:30

  • WhoClassmates and blockmates with morning classes in our building — the ones who leave the house without eating.
  • Why themThey already buy coffee somewhere. We are closer, cheaper than a café, and we are there at the one time they need it.
  • Not yetThe whole campus, walk-in buyers and delivery. Each one needs a cart, a permit, or hours we do not have.
Orders close at 8 PM. We bake and bottle at night, chill it, and hand it over in one 30-minute window the next morning.How it works
Causal · Execution steps

Four Months, One Job Each

  1. September — Launch small and prove the windowMon, Wed, Fri only. Ten bottles and eight cookie packs a day, all pre-paid. One handover spot, one 30-minute window.₱500 a day
  2. October — Fix the product and share the loadLock one coffee recipe and add a second cookie flavour. Rotate who bakes each week so nobody carries it alone.₱650 a day
  3. November — Take orders from one more sectionOpen the order form to the floor above and sell a ₱90 combo. Only grow the list as far as one bag can still cover it.₱800 a day
  4. December — Hold through finals, then decideKeep the three days through exam week, split the profit five ways, and choose together whether AM Brew continues in January.₱6,000 a month

Checked every week: items sold, earnings per item, how many pre-orders we actually filled, and cash on hand — which never goes below ₱1,500.

Risk · What could go wrong

Four Things That Could Kill It

  1. DemandEveryone tries it once, then week three nobody orders.What we do We never buy stock before the orders are in. An empty list costs us a night off, not ₱3,500.
  2. Food safetyOne warm bottle makes somebody sick, and we are finished.What we do Made the night before, kept on ice, handed over by 8. Leftovers are thrown out, never re-sold.
  3. PeopleWe are five students with exams, and one of us will get sick.What we do Two people know every job. A day we cannot cover, we close orders the night before.
  4. MoneyThe ₱3,500 quietly turns into five people's allowance.What we do One person holds the cash and counts it every Friday in front of the other four.

Every fix costs ₱0 — with five people and ₱3,500, not spending is the only insurance we can afford. The fifth risk, permission to sell, is already answered on Wednesday, before any money moves.

Honest gaps

Where the Plan Is Still Thin

  1. The peso figures are our estimates, not quotesNobody has stood in the store with the list yet. One of us prices the real basket before Friday, and slides 03 and 04 get the actual numbers.Fixed before Friday
  2. The ₱6,000 assumes every week is a normal weekWe divided by four weeks and three selling days. Exam week, holidays and cancelled classes do not work that way — so December is the optimistic number, not the expected one.Known, not solved
  3. None of us has baked to a deadline beforeBaking once is not the same as filling 18 paid orders on a school night. The first week tests us, not the product — which is why it is only ten bottles.Week one tests it
  4. One bag is a ceiling we have not costed pastEverything above ₱800 a day needs a second bag and a second person carrying it in. We stop at the ceiling rather than pretend we planned for it.Out of scope

None of these stop us starting on Monday. They are what we check before the ₱3,500 leaves anyone's pocket — and what we would be asked about first.

Recap

We Used Both — in This Order

  1. What do we sell?Effectual: coffee and cookies, because we can already make both at home. Causal: coffee and cookies, because they earn the most per item we carry.
  2. How do we start?Effectual: one post, 20 orders, ₱0 spent until Friday. Causal: nothing — a plan does not tell you what to do on Monday.
  3. When are we done?Effectual: never fixed, whatever our buyers turn it into. Causal: December, ₱6,000 a month — a number we can be wrong about.
