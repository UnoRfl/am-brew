EFFECTUAL · STEP 1

What I Already Have

The ₱5,000 is the smallest of the four.
WHO I AM A student in the same building, five days a week, on the same schedule as my customers. No commute cost, no rent, no permit for a stall I do not have.
WHAT I KNOW Which rooms have 7:30 AM classes, when the long breaks fall, and that the nearest ₱40 iced coffee is a twelve-minute walk off campus.
WHO I KNOW Three class group chats I am already a member of — about 180 people who will read a message from me without me paying for reach.
WHAT THE MONEY IS ACTUALLY FOR ₱5,000 does not buy a business. It buys one week of stock and one piece of equipment — enough to find out whether the other three means are worth anything.
THE EFFECTUAL QUESTION "Given only this, what can I start — this week?"
Effectuation starts from means already in hand, not from a goal that has to be funded first.

EFFECTUAL · STEP 4

The Most I Am Willing to Lose

Not a feeling. A number, with a rule attached.
AFFORDABLE LOSS ₱3,800 of the ₱5,000 — the kit and the first week of stock. If all of it disappears, I have lost two weeks of allowance and no one else's money.
HOW THE ₱5,000 SPLITS
At risk ₱3,800
Untouched ₱1,200
The ₱1,200 is not savings. It is the cash that lets me restock next week without borrowing.
WHAT I WILL NOT RISK
1
Borrowed money No loan, no utang from family, no 5-6. A first business that owes someone is a first business that cannot be closed.
2
Class hours Selling happens in the two breaks I already have free. The moment it needs a third, it waits for the semester break.
3
More than ₱3,800, ever Growth gets funded out of margin. If the business cannot pay for its own next jug, it does not get one.
THE STOP RULE Three selling weeks — nine days. If gross margin has not passed ₱1,900, half of what I risked, I stop, sell the jug, and keep the ₱1,200.
CAUSAL · THE GOAL, WORKED BACKWARD

₱6,000 a Month Is 24 Cups a Day

Fix the goal first, then divide until the number is something I can watch.
THE GOAL ₱6,000 net profit per month, by December 2026
+ FIXED COSTS ₱6,400 gross margin needed — ₱400 covers breakage, transport, replacements
÷ ₱23 A CUP 279 cups a month
÷ 12 SELLING DAYS 24 cups a day, three days a week
IS 24 REALISTIC? There are about 600 students on the three floors I sell to. 24 cups is 4 out of every 100 buying once.
CAN I PHYSICALLY DO IT? Split over two breaks, that is 12 cups per window — one hand-off every 2.5 minutes, all pre-paid.
WHAT BREAKS IT? Margin slipping under ₱21 a cup. At ₱18 I would need 31 cups a day for the same ₱6,000.
Backward math: ₱6,400 ÷ ₱23 = 278.3, rounded up to 279 cups. Four selling weeks a month, three selling days a week.
CAUSAL · THE MARKET

Three Floors, One Building, 7:30 AM

Chosen because I can reach all of it on foot in under two minutes.
WHY THIS MARKET
1
They already buy coffee I am not creating a habit, I am moving one. The 7:30 crowd is already caffeinated by something.
2
The nearest option is a walk Twelve minutes off campus, or ₱60 and up on it. I sell at ₱40, inside the building, before the bell.
3
I can see demand before I spend Pre-orders close at 9 PM. I buy ice and brew for exactly the number of cups already paid for. Nothing spoils.
PRIMARY 2nd to 4th year students with 7:30 AM classes About 600 of them on my three floors. Same rooms every week, so the list stays warm.
SECONDARY Faculty on the same floors Fewer people, bigger ticket — ₱55 for a 22oz. Worth serving, not worth planning around.
NOT MY MARKET — YET The whole campus, walk-in buyers, and delivery. Each one needs a cart, a permit, or hours I do not have while carrying 21 units.
CAUSAL · STRATEGY & EXECUTION

Four Months, Four Numbers to Hit

Each step exists only because the one after it needs it.
STRATEGY

Pre-order only, one building, ₱40 flat — so I never buy stock I have not sold, and I never compete on anything but distance.

01 SEPTEMBER Validate free, then launch. 25 committed names, then Mon–Wed–Fri selling at 30 cups a day. Target · 30 cups/day
02 OCTOBER Add the 3 PM window and the ₱55 large. Fix the recipe so every cup tastes the same. Target · 45 cups/day
03 NOVEMBER Pay one classmate ₱150 a day to run the morning window so I can attend my own 7:30. Target · 60 cups/day
04 DECEMBER Hold the average through finals week, then decide: a second building, or pause until January. Goal · ₱6,000 net
Checked weekly: cups sold, margin per cup, pre-order fill rate, and cash on hand — which never drops below ₱1,200.
RECAP

I Used Both — in This Order

Effectual to survive week one. Causal to be worth something by December.
THE QUESTION EFFECTUAL ANSWERED CAUSAL ANSWERED
What do I sell? Coffee — because I am in the building at 7:30 anyway. Coffee — because ₱23 a cup is the only margin that reaches ₱6,000.
How do I start? One post, 25 names, ₱0 spent. Buy only after they commit. Silent on this. A plan does not tell you what to do on Monday.
How much do I risk? ₱3,800, with a written stop rule at nine selling days. Whatever the ₱6,000 target needs — which is how plans get expensive.
When am I done? Never fixed. Whatever the buyers turn it into. December, ₱6,000 net, 24 cups a day. A number I can be wrong about.
Effectual reasoning got me selling in five days. Causal reasoning is the only reason I know whether selling was worth it.
